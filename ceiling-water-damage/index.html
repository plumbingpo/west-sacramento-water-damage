Ceiling Water Damage Repair in West Sacramento

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. By the time the stain appears, water has already run along a joist, soaked the insulation and pooled on the back of the drywall. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. While the space overhead stays damp, the stain returns, and within 24 to 48 hours mold can begin on the drywall paper. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Signs to Look For

  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • New fine cracks that radiate from a discolored patch
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
  • Drywall that sags, bows or feels soft when pressed gently
  • Discolored rings overhead that get bigger each time it rains or the bathroom is used
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. From inside the room everything can appear normal, since the drywall is taking on water on the hidden face.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

Discoloration shows up and the paint begins bubbling. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Day 3 to 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. A stale odor drifts into the room as seams separate and the drywall sags in the spans between framing.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Travels Sideways

Because moisture runs along framing, plumbing and ductwork, the drip may be feet away from the entry point. We check beyond the visible stain so soaked sections down the line are not overlooked.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Popcorn texture and plaster from past decades may have asbestos in them. Have a sample tested before any scraping, cutting or removal if your ceiling is one of these.

Damage on the Floor Above

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. We check that level as well, because drying only the ceiling leaves moisture behind.

Electricity Overhead

Recessed lights, fans and junction boxes sit right in the wet zone. A short is possible when water enters a light, which is why the breaker should be shut off.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Source

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. Repairs are pointless until that leak is shut down.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Open and Dry

We take down the ruined drywall and the soaked insulation. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
