Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in West Sacramento

Hardwood responds fast when it gets wet. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Signs to Look For

  • Wide spaces between planks once the wood dries too quickly
  • Swollen trim along the edge of the floor near the leak
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Board edges sitting higher than the centers, called cupping
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Drying Too Fast

High heat or too much airflow pulls moisture from the surface faster than the core can follow. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Growth Beneath the Floor

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Damage Beyond the Room

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

Remove Surface Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

The edges come up as each board expands. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.
