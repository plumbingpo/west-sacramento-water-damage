Kitchen Water Damage: What West Sacramento Homeowners Should Know

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in West Sacramento, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Cupped or raised floorboards, wood or laminate, near the dishwasher door
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

Toe Kick Space

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

The Dishwasher Cavity

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

How We Fix It

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry Without Demolition

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
