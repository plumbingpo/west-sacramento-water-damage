Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in West Sacramento

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • Nail or screw heads popping out and seams showing through the paint
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Swollen or warped baseboards with a gap opening behind them
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Walls You Cannot See

Wet drywall behind cabinets, vanities and built-ins gets no airflow and is easy to overlook. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Insulation Holding Water

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

What We Do

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Wicking Begins

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
