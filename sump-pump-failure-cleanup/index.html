Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in West Sacramento

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Heavy rain raises the water table, the pit fills, and then the power goes out, the float sticks or the pump cannot keep up. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. It keeps coming until a pump is running again.

This water is not clean. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Keep Out of the BasementNever wade into basement water before the power has been cut. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelySee if the plug pulled out, the outlet has tripped or the float got stuck on the side of the pit. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.
  • Contents handling, meaning what is stored down there and how much can be kept.
  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.
  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Low spots, the area by the pit and the rest of the floor are cleared of water and silt.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • Discharge piping that has come apart, been flattened or plugged up outdoors.
  • Losing electricity in a storm when the pump has no battery backup.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Boxes and Stored Items

Books, cloth, boxes and wood furniture legs take in dirty water within a short time. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

Carpet and Flooring

Carpet and pad soaked by groundwater cannot be cleaned to a safe standard and are removed. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.

Low Electrical Outlets

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Mechanical equipment often shares the floor with the sump pit. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.
